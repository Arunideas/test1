## Caption

Data literacy is not about becoming the best maths student in the room.

It is about not freezing when work comes with numbers.

In internships, numbers show up quietly:

- A campaign report
- A sales sheet
- A user survey
- A hiring funnel
- A product dashboard

You do not need to solve advanced equations to be useful there.

You need to ask better questions.

Start with three:

1. What changed?
2. What might have caused the change?
3. What decision should we make carefully because of this data?

That is the beginning of data literacy.

Not big formulas.

Not pretending every chart is proof.

Just the ability to read a small set of numbers, notice the pattern, and explain the limit of what you know.

A simple practice for this week:

Pick one spreadsheet, report, or public dashboard.

Write five lines:

- The main number
- The comparison number
- One possible reason
- One thing you cannot conclude yet
- One decision you would check before acting

This is a future skill because AI can create charts quickly.

But someone still has to ask whether the chart is meaningful.

That someone can be you.

What kind of data do you find hardest to read?
