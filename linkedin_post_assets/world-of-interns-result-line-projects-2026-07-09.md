Topic: The result line recruiters look for in student projects.

## Caption

A project on your resume should not stop at what you built.

Recruiters also look for what changed because you built it.

Many student resumes say:

"Built a dashboard using React and Node.js."

That is useful, but incomplete.

A stronger version adds one result line:

"Built a dashboard using React and Node.js to reduce manual tracking for a college club from 2 hours to 20 minutes."

Even if your result is small, it gives context.

Who used it?
What problem did it remove?
How often was it used?
What became faster, clearer, or easier?

You do not need to exaggerate.

You need to connect the work to a real change.

Before your next internship application, pick one project and add one honest result line under it.

Would your strongest project become easier to understand if you added a result line?
