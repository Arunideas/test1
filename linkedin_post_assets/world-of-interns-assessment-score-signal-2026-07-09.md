## Caption

An assessment score is not the full hiring decision.

It is usually the first signal.

When a hiring team looks at intern applicants, the score helps answer a few practical questions:

Can this student handle the basic work?

Is the role fit visible?

Will the interview need to start from zero, or can it go deeper?

The mistake students make is treating an assessment like a school exam.

They focus only on the final number.

Recruiters usually read it with context:

- which skills were tested
- how relevant those skills are to the role
- whether the resume shows the same strengths
- whether the project work supports the score

A good score with no proof can feel incomplete.

A decent score with clear projects, clean communication, and role fit can still be useful.

Before applying, do a simple 60-second check:

Does my resume explain why this score matters for this role?

If not, add one project line or proof point that connects the score to actual work.

That small context makes the signal easier to trust.

What do you think matters more for freshers: the score itself, or the proof around it?
