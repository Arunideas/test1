Post copy:

Data literacy is not the same as being good at maths.

A lot of students avoid dashboards, analytics tasks, or data-heavy roles because they remember formulas more than decisions.

But most entry-level work starts much simpler.

Can you read a table and ask what changed?

Can you spot a number that looks too perfect?

Can you explain a trend without pretending it proves everything?

Can you separate "this increased" from "this caused it"?

That is data literacy.

Before applying for any role, try one small habit this week:

Take one spreadsheet, report, or placement chart.

Write three observations.

Write one question the data cannot answer.

Write one decision you would make only after checking more context.

You do not need to become a statistician to start.

You need to become someone who does not get fooled by a chart.

What is one number you check before trusting a claim?
