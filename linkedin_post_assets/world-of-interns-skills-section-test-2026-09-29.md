## Caption

Your skills section is not a storage room.

It is a filter.

Most fresher resumes list every tool, language, framework, course, and keyword the student has touched once.

That feels safe.

But it makes the recruiter do extra work.

A stronger skills section passes three tests:

1. Is this skill relevant to the role?
2. Is there proof elsewhere on the resume?
3. Can someone scan it in 7 seconds?

For example, "Python, SQL, Excel, Power BI" is useful only if your projects or internship lines show where you used them.

If the proof is missing, the keyword becomes noise.

Try this before sending your next resume:

Pick the 8-12 skills most connected to the role.

Group them clearly.

Then make sure your projects, internships, or coursework prove the top skills.

A short, proven skills section is better than a long, hopeful one.

What would you remove from your resume skills section today?
