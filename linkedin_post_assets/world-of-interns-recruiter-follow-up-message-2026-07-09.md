## Caption

A follow-up message is not meant to pressure a recruiter.

It is meant to make your application easy to place.

That is a different goal.

Many freshers send a follow-up like this:

Any update?

Then they send the same line again after two days.

The problem is not that they followed up.

The problem is that the recruiter still has no context.

Recruiters are usually moving between roles, resumes, calls, approvals, and hiring managers.

So a useful follow-up gives them a small, clean reminder.

It answers four things quickly:

- who you are
- which role you applied for
- why you are relevant
- what you are asking

For example:

Hello [Name], I applied for the frontend intern role last week. I noticed the role mentions React dashboards, so I wanted to share that I built a student placement tracker with React, filters, and form validation. If the role is still open, is there anything else you would like me to send?

This message is not perfect.

But it gives the recruiter something useful.

Role context.

Relevant proof.

A clear next step.

Before sending your next follow-up, remove the pressure and add context.

One calm reminder can work better than three empty pings.
