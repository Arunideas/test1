## Caption

Most students try to memorise the answer.
Strong interns learn to debug how they reached it.

That matters more than it sounds.

In a real internship, nobody hands you questions in syllabus order.
You get a confusing task, partial context, and a result that does not match what you expected.

The useful skill is not pretending to know.
It is slowing down enough to ask:

1. What did I assume?
2. What evidence do I actually have?
3. Where did my reasoning jump?
4. What small test would prove or disprove this?

Try this once this week.

Take a problem you got wrong.
Do not only copy the correct answer.
Write three lines:

- My first assumption was...
- The mistake started when...
- Next time I will test...

That habit makes you better at coding, analysis, interviews, and working with AI.
