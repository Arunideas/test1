Post copy:

Debugging your own thinking is becoming a future skill.

Not debugging code.

Debugging the first answer your mind gives you.

AI tools can draft answers.

Courses can teach syntax.

But at work, a fresher is often judged on something quieter:

Can you notice when your approach is vague, copied, or only half-tested?

This matters in internships.

When you choose a solution without asking "what else could be true?", you miss edge cases.

When you copy an AI answer without checking the assumption, you borrow its mistake.

When you say "I understand" too early, you stop learning at the exact point where work begins.

A simple 3-step thinking debug:

1. State the problem in one line.

2. Name the assumption you are making.

3. Test one opposite possibility.

Example:

"Users are not signing up because the form is long."

Assumption:

Length is the problem.

Opposite check:

Maybe the first field is confusing, or the value is not clear enough.

Same problem.

Better thinking.

This is useful in resumes too.

Instead of writing "improved website performance", explain what you checked, what you changed, and how you knew it worked.

The future skill is not having instant answers.

It is catching weak answers before someone else has to.

Before your next project update, ask:

"What assumption did I not test?"

What helps you think more clearly: writing it down, talking it through, or testing a small version?
