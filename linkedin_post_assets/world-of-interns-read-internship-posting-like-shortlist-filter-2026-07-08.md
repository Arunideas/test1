## Caption

Most students read an internship post like a description.

Recruiters read it like a filter.

That small difference changes the quality of an application.

Before applying, spend 3 minutes marking the posting in three places:

1. The repeated skill.
If "Excel", "research", "content", "React", or "client coordination" appears more than once, your resume should show proof for that skill near the top.

2. The work output.
Look for the thing the intern will actually produce: reports, dashboards, social posts, user research notes, landing pages, test cases, or sales leads.

3. The hidden constraint.
"Fast-paced", "remote", "field work", "immediate joining", or "own laptop" is not filler. It is often a shortlist condition.

Then write one line before you apply:

"This role seems to need someone who can ___, and my closest proof is ___."

If you cannot fill that line honestly, either improve the application or skip the role.

A strong internship application is not longer.
It is easier to shortlist.
