Caption:

Most students treat writing as a soft skill.

Recruiters do not.

In a technical internship, your writing is often the first proof of how you think.

Your resume bullets.
Your project README.
Your GitHub description.
Your follow-up email.
Your answer to "what did you build?"

All of them show whether you can explain messy work clearly.

Good writing does not mean big words.

It means someone can quickly understand:

1. What problem you solved
2. What you decided to build
3. What changed because of it
4. Where they can see proof

A strong project can look weak if the explanation is vague.

An average project can look more credible when the thinking is clear.

Before your next application, open one project and rewrite the description in four plain sentences:

Problem.
Action.
Result.
Proof.

If a recruiter had only 20 seconds, would they still understand why it matters?
