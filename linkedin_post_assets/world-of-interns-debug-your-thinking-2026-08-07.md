## Caption

Memorising gives you speed.

Debugging your own thinking gives you range.

That difference matters more every year.

A student can memorise one answer for an interview.

But the moment the question changes, the script breaks.

The stronger habit is slower at first:

What am I assuming?

What proof do I have?

Which step is weak?

How would I explain the fix to someone else?

This is useful in coding.

It is useful in resume writing.

It is useful in interviews, projects, and workplace feedback.

Because real work rarely arrives in the same format as the notes.

For this week, try one small exercise:

Take one answer, project, or resume bullet you feel confident about.

Do not just repeat it.

Break it.

Check the assumption.

Find the vague part.

Rewrite it so another person can trust the logic.

That is a future skill.

Not because it sounds advanced.

Because it helps you keep learning when the question changes.

What is one area where students should practise this more?
