## Post copy

Most applications are written like a story.
Recruiters scan them like a checklist.

That gap costs good students attention.

A recruiter is usually trying to answer four things quickly:

1. Which role does this student fit?
2. What proof is visible?
3. Is there one result or project outcome?
4. What should I do next?

If your profile opens with a long summary, generic skills, and three unrelated projects, the recruiter has to translate your application.

Make it easier.

Use a clear target line.
Put the most relevant project first.
Add one measurable result, even if small.
End with a direct next step: available for assessment, assignment, or interview.

This is not about sounding impressive.
It is about reducing the recruiter's work.

Which part of your application answers "why this role" the fastest?
