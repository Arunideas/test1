## Caption

Most students treat AI like a shortcut.

Some treat it like a threat.

Both miss the real skill.

In internships, AI will not remove the need to think. It will expose who can think clearly with better tools in front of them.

The useful student is not the one who writes the longest prompt.

It is the one who can:

- ask a clearer question
- check the answer against a real source
- turn rough output into a usable draft
- explain what they accepted and rejected
- notice when the tool is confidently wrong

That is not just prompting.

That is judgement.

A simple practice for this week:

1. Use AI for a first version.
2. Mark three lines you verified.
3. Rewrite one weak answer yourself.
4. Save the before/after with a note on why you changed it.

This small habit builds the skill companies actually notice:

using tools without outsourcing your thinking.

Where should students use AI, but still show their own judgement?
