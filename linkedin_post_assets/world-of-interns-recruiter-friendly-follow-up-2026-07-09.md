Caption:

Most follow-up messages fail because they ask the recruiter to do extra work.

"Any update?" is easy to send.
But it gives no context.

A recruiter may be handling multiple roles, old applications, interview slots, and internal approvals.

The better follow-up makes the decision easy to locate.

Use this simple structure:

1. Context
Mention the role and when you applied or interviewed.

2. One useful proof point
Add one line that connects your work to the role.

3. One clear question
Ask about the next step, not a vague update.

Example:

"Hi, I applied for the Data Analyst Intern role last Thursday.

I have worked on Excel, SQL, and a student dashboard project where I cleaned and visualised survey data.

Could you let me know if there is any next step I should prepare for?"

No pressure.
No long pitch.
No repeated messages every day.

Just enough information for the recruiter to place you quickly.
