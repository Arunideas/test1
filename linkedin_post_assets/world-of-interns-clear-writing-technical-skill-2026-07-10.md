Post copy:

Clear writing is becoming a technical skill.

Not because every student needs to become a content writer.

Because unclear work is expensive.

A recruiter reading your project does not have time to decode what you built.

A manager reviewing your assignment does not want five vague lines around one simple decision.

An interviewer listening to your explanation is checking whether you can think in steps.

The skill is not fancy English.

It is this:

Name the task.

Explain the decision.

Show the result.

For example, do not write:

"Worked on data analysis project."

Write:

"Cleaned 8,000 rows of survey data, removed duplicate entries, and built a dashboard that helped the team compare student feedback by course."

Same project.

Different signal.

One line shows activity.

The other shows judgment.

Before you send your next resume, assignment, or project note, read one paragraph and ask:

"Can someone understand the work without asking me a follow-up question?"

That small check can make your technical skill easier to trust.

Where do you struggle more: explaining the work, or choosing what detail to include?
