## Caption

Clear writing is becoming a technical skill.

Not because every student needs to become a content writer.

Because real work depends on instructions other people can understand.

In an internship, unclear writing creates small delays everywhere:

- A manager has to ask what you meant
- A teammate builds the wrong thing
- A client reads confidence where there is uncertainty
- An AI tool gives a weak answer because the task was vague
- Your own notes stop making sense after two days

Writing clearly is not about big vocabulary.

It is about reducing confusion.

Before you send an update, prompt, email, or project note, check four things:

1. What is the task?
2. What is already done?
3. What is blocked or uncertain?
4. What decision or help is needed next?

That structure is simple.

But it changes how people experience working with you.

You become easier to guide.

You become easier to trust.

You make fewer people guess.

This matters even more in the AI era.

AI can generate text quickly.

But if your starting thought is messy, the output usually becomes polished confusion.

A useful practice for this week:

Take one old message, project update, or AI prompt.

Rewrite it in five lines:

- Context
- Goal
- What you tried
- What is unclear
- Next action needed

That is not fancy writing.

That is work clarity.

And work clarity is a future skill.

Where do you find writing clearly hardest: emails, project updates, or AI prompts?
