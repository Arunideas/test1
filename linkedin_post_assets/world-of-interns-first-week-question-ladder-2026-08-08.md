Post copy:

```text
Your first-week question can decide how much you learn.

Many interns hear a task and reply, "Okay, I will do it."

Then they spend two hours guessing what "good" means.

This is where rework starts.

A better first question is:

"Before I start, what would a good version of this look like?"

Then ask three small follow-ups:

1. Where should I look first?
2. What mistake should I avoid?
3. When should I show a rough version?

This does not make you look weak.

It shows you want to reduce rework.

Internship managers rarely expect you to know everything.

They do notice whether you ask questions that move the work forward.

If you are starting a new internship, write these four prompts in your notes before your next task.

What is one question that helped you understand a task faster?
```
