## Caption

Good marks can show that you understood the chapter.

But an internship asks a slightly different question:

Can you use what you know when the problem is messy?

That gap is where many students get stuck.

Not because they are not smart.

Because college often rewards the final answer, while work rewards the path:

1. Can you define the problem clearly?
2. Can you choose the right concept without being told?
3. Can you test whether your answer works?
4. Can you explain what you changed when it did not?

This is why practical skill is not separate from theory.

It is theory under pressure.

A small way to build it this week:

Take one concept you learned recently.

Do not just revise it.

Use it to make something small:

- a spreadsheet
- a short script
- a case note
- a dashboard mockup
- a before-and-after resume fix

Then write three lines:

What did I apply?
Where did I get stuck?
What would I try differently next time?

That is how theory turns into evidence.

And evidence is what helps a student sound ready before they have years of experience.

What is one concept from your course that you could turn into a small proof-of-skill project?
