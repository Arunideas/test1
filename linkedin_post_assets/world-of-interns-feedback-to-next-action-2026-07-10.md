Topic: Friday Future Skill - turning feedback into a smaller next action.

## Caption

Feedback is not useful when it only stays as a feeling.

"Good work."

"Needs more depth."

"Improve communication."

Most students hear these lines and try to fix everything at once.

That is where the learning gets messy.

One future skill worth building is the ability to translate feedback into one small next action.

Try this after a project review, mock interview, resume check, or internship task:

1. Write the exact feedback sentence.
2. Underline the part you can control.
3. Turn it into one visible action for the next attempt.

"Needs more depth" becomes:

Add one example.

Show one trade-off.

Explain one decision.

"Improve communication" becomes:

Send a shorter update.

Ask the question earlier.

Confirm the deadline in writing.

This habit matters because work will keep changing.

Tools will change.

Roles will change.

But the ability to take unclear feedback, reduce it, and act on it will stay useful.

Before your next submission, ask:

What is the one action this feedback is really asking for?
