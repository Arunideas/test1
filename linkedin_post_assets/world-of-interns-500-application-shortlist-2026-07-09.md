## Caption

When 500 applications land, recruiters do not read like teachers.

They scan for reasons to keep you in the pile.

Not reasons to admire every line.

Most students write for a patient reader.

A long summary.
A skills section with no proof.
A project title that needs explanation.
A resume where the strongest point is hidden halfway down the page.

In a crowded internship opening, the first pass is usually simple:

1. Is the role fit clear?
2. Is the skill proof visible?
3. Is the latest work easy to understand?
4. Is there anything that creates risk?

Before you apply, do a 60-second shortlist check.

Put the target role near the top.
Pair each important skill with one project or result.
Make dates, education, and latest work easy to find.
Remove claims you cannot explain in an interview.

If your resume needs you beside it to make sense, it may not survive the first scan.

Make the useful part obvious.

Which part of your resume would a recruiter notice first today?
