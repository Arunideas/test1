## Caption

A project section is not stronger because it has more tech names.

It gets stronger when a recruiter can see three things quickly:
what you built, what you owned, and what proof exists.

Many fresher resumes stop at the stack.

"React, Node, MongoDB project."

That tells the tools.
It does not tell the work.

Before sending your resume, check every project line for five details:

- problem
- your role
- working feature
- real metric or scope
- link, demo, or repo

If you do not have usage numbers, use honest scope.

Screens built.
APIs written.
Dataset size.
Deployment status.
Test cases.

A simple format helps:

Built [what] for [who/problem].
Owned [part].
Proved it with [real result or scope].

No fake numbers.
No inflated claims.
Just enough proof for a recruiter to keep reading.

Which project line on your resume needs this check?

-- World of Interns
