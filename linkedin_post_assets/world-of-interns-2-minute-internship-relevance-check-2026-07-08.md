## Caption

Before you apply, check if the internship can explain the work.

A useful listing usually answers 3 things clearly:

1. What problem will the intern work on?
2. Which skills will be used every week?
3. Who will review the work?

If the post only says "great exposure", "dynamic team", and "certificate provided", pause.

That does not always mean the role is fake.

But it does mean you need one extra question before sending your resume:

"What will my first two weeks of work look like?"

Good internships can answer that in plain language.

Weak ones usually avoid the question.

A 2-minute relevance check can save you from applying to roles that were never clear in the first place.
