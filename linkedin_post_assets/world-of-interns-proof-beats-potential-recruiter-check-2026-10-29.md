Caption:

Good candidates do get rejected.

Not always because they lack skill.

Often because the recruiter cannot see the proof quickly enough.

A resume or LinkedIn profile may say:

"Hardworking."
"Quick learner."
"Good communication skills."

But a recruiter is scanning for evidence:

What role are you aiming for?

Which project, task, internship, or course proves you can do that work?

What changed because of your effort?

What should they read next?

Potential is useful.

Proof is faster to trust.

Before you send your next application, replace one vague line with one specific line.

Not "worked on a project."

Try:

"Built a student feedback dashboard using Excel and presented weekly insights to a 5-member team."

It does not need to sound impressive.

It needs to be clear.

Where do you usually find proof on a student's profile first?
