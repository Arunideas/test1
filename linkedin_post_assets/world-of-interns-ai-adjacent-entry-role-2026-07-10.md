## Caption

Many students hear "AI career" and imagine only model building.

But many first AI-adjacent roles look more practical than that.

You may not be training a model.

You may be helping a team use AI safely inside real work.

That can mean:
1. Turning a messy process into clear steps.
2. Checking AI output against the source material.
3. Explaining where the answer is useful, risky, or incomplete.

This is why prompt skills alone are not enough.

The stronger signal is judgment.

Can you understand the task before asking AI?

Can you catch a confident wrong answer?

Can you turn a rough output into something a manager, recruiter, customer, or team member can actually use?

If you are a student, build one small proof project:

Pick a real workflow around you.

Summarise internship listings, compare course reviews, clean survey responses, or draft FAQ answers.

Then show:
- the original problem
- the AI-assisted workflow
- the checks you used
- the final output

That is closer to entry-level AI work than simply collecting prompts.

The opportunity is not "AI will do everything."

The opportunity is learning how to make AI useful, checked, and clear.

Which everyday student workflow would you turn into a small AI-assisted project?
