## Caption

One small intern habit saves a lot of rework:

Share the rough version early.

Not the messy version you have not checked.

Not the final version after three days of silence.

The useful middle version.

The version where your manager can still say:

"This direction is right."

Or:

"Change the audience."

Or:

"Cut this part before you spend more time on it."

Many first-time interns wait until something feels perfect before showing it.

That feels safe.

But it can quietly create more risk.

You may polish the wrong structure.

You may answer the wrong question.

You may miss one simple expectation that could have been fixed in five minutes.

A better habit:

1. Clarify the task
2. Build a small first version
3. Share what you are unsure about
4. Ask, "Is this direction useful?"

This does not make you look underprepared.

It makes your work easier to guide.

And in an internship, being easy to guide is a real strength.

What is one task where interns should ask for feedback earlier?
