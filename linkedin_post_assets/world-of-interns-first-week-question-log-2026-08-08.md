## Topic

Saturday Student Story: the first-week question log interns can use to ask for help without sounding unprepared.

## Caption

Many interns think asking questions makes them look unprepared.

So they stay quiet.

Then they guess.

That is where a small doubt becomes avoidable rework.

A useful first-week question has three parts:

1. What I understood
2. What I already tried
3. The specific choice I need checked

Instead of:

"I am confused. What should I do?"

Try:

"I understood that this report is for the sales team. I checked last week's version and the sheet names. Should I use the same format, or is there a new template?"

Good questions do not ask someone else to think for you.

They show that you have already started thinking.

In your first internship, your manager does not expect you to know everything.

They do expect you to make your confusion visible early.

That habit builds trust faster than pretending to be confident.

If you are joining a new team, keep a small question log:

- task
- what you tried
- blocker
- decision needed

What is one question you wish you had asked earlier in your first week?
