## Post copy

Recruiters do not read applications in the order students write them.

They scan for fit first.

Role title.
Relevant project.
Tools used.
One proof of outcome.
Availability.
Location or remote fit.

If those signals are hidden in paragraph four, the application looks weaker than it is.

This is why a shorter, clearer note often beats a longer "passionate and hardworking" one.

Before you apply, do a 60-second scan of your own profile:

Can someone understand the role you want in one line?
Can they see one relevant project without scrolling?
Can they tell why this internship matches your current skills?

Small clarity fixes do not guarantee a callback.

But they stop good work from being missed in the first screen.

What is one detail you now check before sending an application?
