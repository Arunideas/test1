Your skills section should not look like a keyword dump.

Recruiters do not read "Python, Excel, SQL, Communication, Leadership..." and instantly know what you can do.

They look for proof.

A simple check:

1. Keep 8-12 relevant skills.
2. Group them by type.
3. Attach proof somewhere else in the resume.
4. Remove any skill you cannot discuss for two minutes.

Example:

Instead of only writing "Excel", add a project bullet:

"Cleaned a 3,000-row placement dataset in Excel and built a simple dashboard for department trends."

The skills section helps scanning.

The project bullet builds trust.

If your resume has 30 skills but no proof, it looks weaker than 10 skills backed by work.

Which skill on your resume needs better proof?
